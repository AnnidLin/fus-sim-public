# CT-informed full-wave time reversal improves focal localization in a controlled transcranial ultrasound simulation workflow

## Abstract

Subject-specific transcranial ultrasound simulations require both anatomical modelling and numerical controls because skull-induced phase aberration can displace the dominant pressure maximum from an intended intracranial target. Here, we evaluated a frozen CT-informed computational configuration (Case079) at 250 kHz using k-Wave. A matched baseline field was compared with a full-wave time-reversal refocusing chain. The prespecified primary endpoints were the target-to-global-peak pressure ratio and the distance between the global pressure peak and the target. We further compared matched PML12 and PML16 fields on their common physical domain. Time reversal increased the target-to-global-peak ratio from 0.4774 to 0.9849 and reduced the peak-to-target distance from 57.38 to 2.49 mm. The simulated pressure magnitude at the target increased from 1.891 to 2.554 MPa, whereas the global maximum decreased from 3.962 to 2.593 MPa. PML12-to-PML16 relative field differences were 1.83 × 10−5 (L2) and 6.75 × 10−5 (L∞) for the baseline, and 5.05 × 10−5 (L2) and 1.46 × 10−4 (L∞) after time reversal; the peak-to-target distances were unchanged. In a separate UCL H115 water comparison, the effective-source dx0.50- and dx0.30-mm models each passed 6/8 frozen endpoints, whereas the nominal equal-amplitude source passed 0/8; the external comparison remained nonpassing. These results show improved simulated focal localization in one frozen computational case and insensitivity of this conclusion to the tested PML perturbation. They do not establish numerical convergence, absolute-pressure accuracy, cross-subject generalization, efficacy or safety.

## Introduction

Transcranial ultrasound can deliver acoustic energy to deep brain targets, but propagation through the skull introduces spatially heterogeneous phase delays, attenuation and refraction. These effects can reduce pressure at the intended target or relocate the dominant pressure maximum. Subject-specific numerical models are therefore used to estimate intracranial fields and to design aberration-correction strategies. However, a favourable simulated focus is not by itself evidence of predictive accuracy: the result also depends on acoustic-property assignment, transducer positioning, spatial and temporal discretization, boundary treatment and the definition of the reported endpoint.

Reporting and benchmark efforts have made these requirements increasingly explicit. The ITRUSST reporting consensus identifies the transducer and drive settings, free-field and estimated in-situ quantities, pulse timing and intensity parameters needed for interpretable and reproducible transcranial ultrasound studies [1]. Complementary intercomparison benchmarks show the value of frozen geometries, material definitions and comparison domains when evaluating transcranial acoustic solvers [2]. Together, these efforts motivate workflows in which physical assumptions, numerical settings and comparison rules are traceable before outcome interpretation.

Time-reversal or phase-reversal approaches offer a direct route to compensating phase aberration in a known computational medium. Their expected physical behaviour nevertheless creates a methodological challenge: demonstrating improved focusing in a single favourable image is less informative than showing that the comparison was matched, the endpoints were predefined and the result survives controlled numerical perturbations. The evidential question is therefore not only whether refocusing occurs, but whether the observed localization change can be reconstructed and bounded.

Here, we report a frozen, CT-informed Case079 simulation workflow that compares a baseline field with full-wave time-reversal refocusing at 250 kHz. We quantify focal localization using the target-to-global-peak ratio and peak-to-target distance, retain simulated pressure magnitude as a secondary model-specific endpoint, and evaluate one controlled boundary-layer perturbation using paired PML12 and PML16 simulations. The scope is deliberately narrow: this study tests an auditable single-case computational result and does not claim experimental validation, formal numerical convergence or clinical performance.

## Methods

### Study scope and evidence level

This was a retrospective computational analysis of one frozen CT-informed acoustic model, identified as Case079. The source image was subject 079 from the PhysioNet CT-ICH v1.3.1 dataset (DOI: 10.13026/4nae-zg36). The dataset record reports ethics approval by the Iraqi Ministry of Health, Babil Office (approval 1369), anonymization of sensitive patient information and facial de-identification of the released NIfTI images. Access is governed by the PhysioNet Restricted Health Data License and Data Use Agreement; the raw CT is therefore not redistributed. The simulations were generated under the project `standard` quality preset, whose machine-readable metadata classifies the evidence as `engineering_validation_not_full_reproduction` and explicitly records `is_paper_grade=false`. No biological, clinical or safety endpoint was evaluated. The analysis plan, comparison matrix, execution records and post-run gap feedback were retained in the project evidence trail.

### Acoustic model and transducer configuration

The acoustic model used the frozen binary `simple_hu300` engineering profile. Resampled CT values below -500 HU were assigned to the coupling/background class, values from -500 to below 300 HU to soft tissue and values at or above 300 HU to skull. The corresponding `(sound speed, density, attenuation prefactor)` values were `(1500 m s−1, 1000 kg m−3, 0.002)`, `(1540 m s−1, 1040 kg m−3, 0.6)` and `(2800 m s−1, 1900 kg m−3, 8.0)`, respectively; attenuation used a power of 1.5, so the prefactor unit was dB/(MHz^1.5 cm). This exact combined profile was retained for backward-compatible matched comparisons and was not treated as a validated subject-specific calibration. CT-informed heterogeneous acoustic mapping has experimental support, but the HU-to-velocity relation is scanner- and reconstruction-dependent [10,11]; neither source directly validates the frozen binary constants. The original CT spacing was 0.390625 × 0.390625 × 5.0 mm before resampling, and no ground-truth skull mask or expert segmentation sign-off was available. The simulation grid spacing was 0.7500000065 mm. The source was a bowl transducer with a 60-mm aperture and a 74.435-mm radius, represented by 4,971 source points. A 250-kHz, two-cycle tone burst with a prescribed source amplitude of 1 MPa was used. The minimum number of grid points per wavelength, evaluated at the minimum sound speed, was 8. Transducer placement and target definition were inherited from the frozen Case079 entry plan. The target was an engineering geometric candidate rather than an anatomically registered or clinically selected target; no Case079 multimodal registration was performed.

### Full-wave simulation

Simulations were executed with Python k-Wave 0.6.1 under Python 3.12.10 on Windows 11 using the CPU backend. The reference grid contained 133 × 98 × 101 voxels, the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy number was 0.2, and the 100-µs simulation used 1,867 time steps with a time step of 5.3571429 × 10−8 s. At every voxel, the stored pressure magnitude was calculated as max(|p_max|, |p_min|)/10^6 MPa from the recorded temporal extrema. Consequently, all pressure values in this article are simulated temporal peak magnitudes rather than experimentally validated in-situ pressures.

The matched comparison comprised a baseline simulation and a full-wave time-reversal chain. The acoustic model, entry plan, source geometry, frequency, prescribed amplitude, grid, simulation window and metric extraction were frozen between conditions. Time-reversal calibration generated the refocusing waveform used in the final simulation. Full commands and machine-readable configurations are preserved in the run artifacts.

### Outcome definitions

The primary localization endpoints were (1) the pressure magnitude at the target divided by the global pressure maximum and (2) the Euclidean distance between the global-peak voxel and target voxel. Secondary endpoints were pressure magnitude at the target and the global pressure maximum. Target and peak indices were extracted from the three-dimensional stored field using metric semantics version 2.0.0. No statistical population inference was performed because the study contained one anatomy and deterministic matched simulations.

### PML sensitivity analysis

The PML size was perturbed from 12 to 16 grid points for both baseline and time-reversal conditions while the remaining frozen configuration was held constant. Because changing the PML altered the cropped array origin and x dimension, fields were aligned in the full-model index frame using `crop_origin_ijk` and compared only on their overlapping physical domain. The PML12 domain was fully covered by the aligned PML16 field. We calculated the relative L2 field difference, relative L∞ field difference, maximum and mean absolute differences, and changes in scalar localization endpoints. This constitutes a paired PML sensitivity observation, not a grid-, time-step- or total-domain-convergence study.

### Reproducibility and execution monitoring

Each accepted simulation unit retained its configuration summary, focus metrics, compressed pressure field, execution status, device status and monitor status. File hashes link the figure panels and scalar results to the accepted source artifacts. The PML16 baseline and refocusing chain required approximately 7.49 h of solver wall time. During the PML16 time-reversal chain, maximum monitored CPU usage was 0.997 equivalent cores, maximum working set was 1.78 GiB and minimum free system memory was 18.49 GiB. An initial CFL0.1 attempt was interrupted by a planned Windows Update restart before core outputs were produced and was excluded rather than resumed or imputed. A fresh recovery completed in an independent output directory.

### Independent H115 water comparison and grid sensitivity

As a separate method-validation branch, an openly licensed UCL H115 270-kHz qCW dataset accompanying the cranial-bone comparison study was compared with a homogeneous-water effective-bowl model [5,8]. One linear amplitude factor was fitted only to the measured axial spatial peak; axial shape and position plus the independent transverse plane remained held out under prospectively frozen limits. The initial dx0.50-mm calculation used 10.98 PPW, PML16 and CFL0.10. A single dx0.30-mm refinement used 18.30 PPW and PML27 to retain approximately 8 mm physical PML thickness, with the full physical domain preserved. The refined field was trilinearly aligned to dx0.50 coordinates relative to each stored source centre and compared after excluding the frozen 5-mm nearfield. A subsequent source-model contract compared the paper-derived effective geometry (61.39-mm radius, 50.40-mm diameter) with the HDF5-documented nominal 64-mm-radius/64-mm-diameter equal-amplitude union in exactly one dx0.50-mm propagation. Registration offsets from -2.5 to +2.5 mm and circular amplitude-domain averaging diameters of 0, 0.2 and 0.5 mm were frozen as secondary sensitivities, consistent with hydrophone-measurement guidance on spatial averaging and positioning uncertainty [9], and could not replace primary endpoints. Full grid, time-step, source-mask, runtime and device parameters are reported in Supplementary Table 2. This branch does not directly validate the Case079 source, skull model or pressure estimates.

## Results

### Time reversal relocated the dominant simulated pressure maximum towards the target

The baseline field placed the global pressure maximum 57.38 mm from the intended target (Fig. 2a). Following time reversal, the global maximum was 2.49 mm from the target and spatially overlapped the target region in the maximum projection (Fig. 2b). The target-to-global-peak ratio increased from 0.4774 to 0.9849, an absolute increase of 0.5075 and a relative increase of 106.30% (Fig. 3a). Peak-to-target distance decreased by 54.89 mm, corresponding to a relative reduction of 95.66% (Fig. 3b).

### Refocusing redistributed the simulated peak-magnitude field

The simulated temporal peak magnitude at the target increased from 1.891 to 2.554 MPa (+35.04%), whereas the global pressure maximum decreased from 3.962 to 2.593 MPa (−34.54%; Fig. 3c). Thus, in this model, the localization improvement reflected both an increase at the target and a reduction of the off-target global maximum. These pressure values are numerical model outputs and should not be interpreted as validated intracranial exposure estimates.

### The localization conclusion was insensitive to the tested PML perturbation

Aligned PML12 and PML16 fields differed only slightly in both conditions (Fig. 4). For the baseline field, relative L2 and L∞ differences were 1.83 × 10−5 and 6.75 × 10−5, respectively. For the time-reversal field, the corresponding differences were 5.05 × 10−5 and 1.46 × 10−4. The baseline peak-to-target distance remained 57.38 mm and the time-reversal value remained 2.49 mm at both PML levels. The target-to-global-peak ratios were likewise unchanged at the reported precision. The tested PML perturbation therefore did not alter the single-case localization conclusion.

### H115 grid refinement preserved the simulated field but not measurement agreement

The dx0.30-mm H115 calculation completed in 6.41 h with all device and artifact gates satisfied. Across 4,377,600 nearfield-excluded common voxels, its source-aligned field agreed closely with dx0.50 (correlation 0.999881; least-squares-scaled relative L2 1.310%; relative L∞ 2.170% of the reference peak; peak change +0.0307%; discrete axial-peak shift 0 mm; Supplementary Fig. 1). A regression audit corrected raster spacing inference in transverse line-width extraction: y-width errors are +5.989% at dx0.50 and +7.160% at dx0.30, not the previously reported +26.608% and +28.144%. The two remaining nonpassing endpoints are axial peak position (1.856 and 2.177 mm) and transverse normalized L2 (15.004% and 15.469%; Supplementary Table 1). Grid refinement therefore did not explain the experimental mismatch.

### Nominal H115 geometry was rejected by a frozen source-model sensitivity

The single nominal-geometry calculation completed in 2,806 s with all resource and artifact gates satisfied (maximum 5.974 equivalent CPU cores, 1.220 GiB working set and 17.282 GiB minimum free system memory). Relative to the effective-bowl dx0.50 reference, nominal geometry increased axial peak-position error from 1.856 to 10.028 mm, axial normalized L2 from 3.780% to 31.030%, and transverse normalized L2 from 15.004% to 118.286% (Supplementary Fig. 2). All preregistered source-explanation thresholds were exceeded in the worsening direction. For the effective bowl, a descriptive +1.0-mm origin offset reduced peak-position error to 0.858 mm while axial L2 remained 5.745%; this arm did not replace the unshifted result. Approximate 0.2-mm circular averaging changed transverse L2 from 15.004% to 15.039%, indicating that aperture averaging at the documented active diameter does not explain the mismatch.

## Discussion

In this frozen Case079 configuration, full-wave time reversal produced a large change in the location and relative dominance of the simulated focus. The global maximum moved from a skull-proximal/off-target region to within 2.49 mm of the intended target, while the target approached the global maximum in magnitude. Because the comparison held the model, source geometry, entry plan and numerical configuration constant, the observed contrast is attributable to the refocusing waveform within this computational system.

The paired PML analysis strengthens a narrow aspect of this interpretation. Increasing the PML from 12 to 16 grid points produced field differences several orders of magnitude below the reference-field magnitude and left both localization distances unchanged. Halving the baseline CFL from 0.2 to 0.1 produced field L2/L∞ differences of 0.654%/1.375%, changed target pressure by −0.770%, and left peak-to-target distance unchanged. This supports baseline time-step stability under the tested perturbation, but not TR time-step robustness or formal convergence. For the Case079 CT branch specifically, grid refinement and expanded-domain tests remain absent, and material-property uncertainty was not evaluated; the separate H115 water-grid study does not close those gaps.

The main scientific limitation is the single anatomy. Time reversal is expected to compensate phase errors when the computational medium used for calibration matches the forward model. The present result therefore demonstrates an auditable implementation and a large within-case contrast, not general effectiveness. Independent cases, including failures, are required to estimate the distribution of localization improvement and to identify anatomical or geometric limits. Recent subject-specific simulations illustrate how phase-reversal performance can instead be evaluated across a cohort [3]. Experimental comparison is also needed before absolute pressure or spatial accuracy can be treated as predictive rather than simulated.

The use of k-Wave is supported by quantitative validation in water and layered absorbing media, where pressure, focus size and focal position were compared directly with calibrated hydrophone measurements [4]. More recent work has extended this validation approach to CT-informed simulations through ex vivo human cranial bones [5]. These studies support the general modelling approach but do not validate the present Case079 source, acoustic mapping or geometry. This distinction is essential: validation evidence is applicable only to the extent that the comparator conditions represent the present context of use.

Our H115 comparison reinforces that distinction. The near-identity of the two simulated grids alongside two persistent held-out measurement failures makes dx0.50 discretization an unlikely primary explanation. The nominal equal-amplitude geometry was decisively worse, supporting retention of the paper-derived effective bowl, while approximate averaging over the documented 0.2-mm hydrophone aperture had negligible effect. A 1-mm descriptive origin shift could satisfy the axial peak gate, but applying it without independent uncertainty evidence would be post-hoc registration. Per-ring acoustic output remains unavailable, and pressure-max amplitude averaging is not a complex-pressure hydrophone model; both limitations are material under hydrophone reporting guidance [9]. Because only two grids were evaluated, the water result is a sensitivity observation rather than formal asymptotic convergence. It remains a nonpassing external source-model comparison and all unfavourable endpoints are retained.

Our own ITRUSST Phase-I comparison was likewise mixed rather than uniformly successful [2]. The free-field BM1 case agreed closely with the official k-Wave reference (3.26% relative L2 error, 0.47% focal-pressure difference and 0.5-mm focal-position difference). BM3 retained a 15.77% full-field L2 error and a 2.0-mm focal-position difference despite a 0.23% peak-pressure difference; post-hoc decomposition localized most disagreement to regions below −12 dB rather than the main lobe. BM4 showed larger differences (27.10% full-field L2, 13.53% focal-pressure and 1.5-mm focal-position differences). A fitted global gain explained part, but not all, of the BM4 discrepancy. These fitted diagnostics were not used to replace the unaligned benchmark metrics. Accordingly, the benchmark evidence supports basic source implementation while identifying unresolved layered-medium disagreement that limits stronger verification claims.

Input uncertainty is an additional limitation. Prior work shows that simulated transcranial fields can be strongly affected by skull sound speed, thickness, image smoothing and other acoustic-property-map choices [6], and uncertainty-propagation methods have been proposed specifically for transcranial simulations [7]. Direct measurements further show that CT photon energy and reconstruction method alter the HU-to-acoustic-velocity relationship [11]. The current `simple_hu300` result samples one uncalibrated binary mapping rather than an uncertainty distribution, uses threshold-only skull segmentation on CT with 5-mm source slice spacing, and targets an engineering coordinate without anatomical registration. Accordingly, the relative baseline–TR contrast is more defensible than its absolute pressure values or anatomical interpretation.

The current work is best interpreted as a reproducible computational-methods case study. Its value lies in coupling a clear localization endpoint with frozen inputs, controlled perturbation, interruption handling and artifact-level traceability. A stronger study should apply the prospectively frozen tolerances and then add matched CFL, grid and domain sensitivities, followed by independent anatomies. The public CT and hydrophone dataset accompanying the 270-kHz-to-1-MHz ex vivo study [5] offers a practical method-level validation route at a frequency close to the present 250 kHz, but would not constitute direct validation of Case079. Such additions should be treated as attempts to falsify the current conclusion rather than opportunities to tune for a more favourable endpoint.
